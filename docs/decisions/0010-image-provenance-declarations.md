# 0010: Which image provenance claims count as "Made with AI"

**Status:** accepted (2026-11-10)

## Context

An image file can say it is AI-made in several ways, with very different strength: a C2PA manifest signed by a trusted certificate, a C2PA manifest signed by an unknown certificate, an unsigned IPTC "trainedAlgorithmicMedia" code in XMP metadata, or settings left behind by an image generator. Manifests can also be broken: the pixels changed after signing, or the signature does not verify.

## Decision

| What the file contains                                                                                                                                                                             | Outcome        | Shown reason                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Intact C2PA manifest, trusted signer, declares AI                                                                                                                                                  | Made with AI   | Signature says an AI tool made it                                                               |
| Intact C2PA manifest, signer not recognised, declares AI                                                                                                                                           | Made with AI   | Same, plus "we do not recognise who signed it"                                                  |
| Intact C2PA manifest whose validated **history** (a parent, or a component placed into it) declares AI, even if the newest step does not                                                           | Made with AI   | "Its digital signature records that an AI tool made or changed it at an earlier step"           |
| Manifest whose content is intact but whose **signer does not meet the standard** (certificate missing the required C2PA usage, expired, or an optional record that cannot be decoded), declares AI | Made with AI   | Same, plus "the signature does not fully meet the standard, so we cannot confirm who signed it" |
| Unsigned IPTC AI code in XMP, or generator settings                                                                                                                                                | Made with AI   | "The file says it was generated by AI" / "contains settings left by an AI image tool"           |
| Intact C2PA manifest, no AI declaration                                                                                                                                                            | No clear signs | "It has a digital signature that does not mention AI"                                           |
| Broken C2PA manifest (content hash, claim signature or a referenced record does not match), whatever it claims                                                                                     | No clear signs | "The signature is broken or changed"                                                            |
| Nothing                                                                                                                                                                                            | No clear signs | none                                                                                            |

- Unsigned and unrecognised-signer declarations are accepted because the realistic risk is small: people rarely label a real photo as AI. The reason text always says which kind of evidence it was.
- A broken manifest proves nothing either way, so its claims are ignored. The simple metadata scan only reads the AI code inside XMP packets, never inside the manifest, so a tampered manifest cannot count through the back door (a test covers this).
- An intact manifest with no AI claim is **not** shown as "real" or "authentic" (ADR-0002). It is context only.
- Validation runs offline with the official C2PA library (`@contentauth/c2pa-node` in Node; `@contentauth/c2pa-web` planned for the extension). Remote manifests are not fetched (ADR-0003).

## Consequences

- Someone can falsely label a real photo as AI by adding metadata. Accepted for now; revisit if it is seen in practice.
- No trust list is loaded yet, so every signer is currently "not recognised". Loading the official C2PA trust list is a follow-up within M3.
- Composite images (a photo with an AI-generated part) are reported the same as fully generated ones. Telling them apart is a later refinement.

## Amendment (2026-11-11): follow the history

Real Gemini downloads showed that the newest manifest is often not where the AI claim is. Google wraps the image in a later "opened, transcoded" manifest whose own actions say only `algorithmicallyEnhanced`; the "Created by Google Generative AI" claim sits in the parent manifest. Reading only the newest manifest wrongly gave _No clear signs_ for a signed, trusted Gemini image.

The summary now walks the history: the newest manifest, then the manifests of ingredients related as `parentOf` (what the image was made from) or `componentOf` (parts placed into it), up to 8 steps deep. Rules:

- Only ingredient manifests that passed validation count. A failed ingredient's claims are ignored, like a broken top-level manifest.
- `inputTo` ingredients (prompts, reference images) are not followed: an AI reference image does not make the result AI-made.
- The reason text says when the claim came from an earlier step, and the evidence keeps the generator's own words (for example "Applied imperceptible SynthID watermark").

Tests: built fixtures for a re-saved AI image (parent) and an AI reference image (input); synthetic stores for a failed ingredient, a component, and a loop; and, locally only, the two real Gemini downloads.

## Amendment 2 (2026-11-11): tampering versus a signer that does not meet the standard

A real Microsoft Copilot (Designer) image is marked invalid by the official validator, but not because anything was changed. The content hash matches. What fails is Microsoft's certificate ("certificate missing required EKU": it lacks the usage flag the current standard requires) and an optional watermark-lookup record that cannot be decoded. Inside, Microsoft declares "Created image using artificial intelligence technology" and "Generated with AI" (Azure OpenAI ImageGen). Treating every failure as tampering gave _No clear signs_ for an image its maker labels as AI.

Validation failures are now split:

| Kind                                                          | Codes                                                                                                                                                          | Effect                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Conformance (who signed, or an optional record we do not use) | `signingCredential.invalid`, `signingCredential.expired`, `claim.malformed` / `assertion.malformed` on an optional assertion (not actions, not a hash binding) | State `unverified`. AI claims count, like unsigned metadata, with the "cannot confirm who signed it" note. Never shown as a good signature. |
| Integrity (anything else, including unknown codes)            | e.g. `assertion.dataHash.mismatch`, `claimSignature.mismatch`, `assertion.hashedURI.mismatch`, ingredient mismatches                                           | State `invalid`. Claims ignored, as before.                                                                                                 |

Unknown codes default to integrity, the safe side. The same split applies to ingredients in the history walk. Revoked certificates stay in the integrity group (a revoked key may be in someone else's hands).

Effect on the official test images: the Nikon photo, whose only failure in the current library is an expired certificate, moves from `invalid` to `unverified`. It makes no AI claim, so its outcome stays _No clear signs_.
